The task

Record the sentiment the writer is expressing

Each sentence comes from a tweet by one of 20 Nepali politicians, journalists or activists, writing about politics, governance and current affairs. You read one sentence and choose one of six answers. That is the whole job.

Judge the sentiment expressed in the sentence — not whether you agree with it, and not whether the underlying event is good or bad news. A sentence can report a terrible event in flat, factual language. That sentence is neutral.

For example

भूकम्पमा परी तीन जनाको मृत्यु भएको छ ।

Terrible news, stated as plain reporting with no evaluative language. The answer is Neutral, not Strongly Negative.

The scale

Six answers

Five points running from most positive to most negative, plus an answer for sentences you cannot judge. On a computer, keys 1 to 6 select them in this order.

Rules that matter

Three things that decide whether the data is usable

Do not discuss sentences with the other annotators

Not while the work is running — not to check a hard one, not to settle a disagreement. The value of this dataset comes from three genuinely independent judgements per sentence. How often you agree is the measurement. It stops being a measurement the moment the answers influence each other.

“Don't know” is a real answer, not a failure

Sentences are split at the danda (।), so some arrive as fragments of two or three words that genuinely cannot be judged alone. Choose Don't know rather than guessing. A guessed label is worse for the dataset than no label, and Don't know is paid exactly the same as any other answer.

It should still be uncommon. The sentences were filtered and prepared so that the large majority are judgeable by a native speaker, so expect to reach for this occasionally, not routinely. If you find yourself using it constantly, stop and tell the coordinator — something is wrong that is worth fixing early.

Trust your first reading

These are ordinary sentences read by a native speaker, not puzzles. If a sentence needs a minute of analysis, that is usually a sign it belongs in Don't know.

Working

How it runs

You will be given a web address, your annotator number (1, 2 or 3) and a password. It works on a phone — add it to your home screen and it opens like an app.

Everything saves as you tap

Each answer is recorded the moment you choose it. Stop whenever you like and reopen later, on any device, and you resume at your next unanswered sentence.

You can go back and change an answer

Use Back and Next to move between sentences. Choosing a different answer replaces the old one. You sign in once — the session lasts 60 days.

The first load of the day is slow

The server sleeps when nobody is using it and takes up to a minute to wake. That is normal, not a fault. Everything after that is immediate.

Checking

Your work is compared with the other two

Once all three of you have finished, the three sets of answers are compared. This is partly the point of the research — how often independent readers agree is the thing being measured — and partly a check that the work was done properly.

Work that fails the check is not paid

An annotator whose answers diverge from both of the others far beyond the normal range, or show long runs of a single label, or arrive faster than the sentences could have been read, is not paid at all. Ask the coordinator what the threshold is before you start — you are entitled to know what you are being held to.

Disagreeing with the others is not failing

Three people will not agree on every sentence, and nobody expects them to. Being consistently a little stricter than the others — Strongly Negative where they say Negative — is a normal, usable pattern, not a problem. What the check looks for is answers that do not look like reading at all.

Do not try to guess what the others would say

It is the wrong strategy and it damages the dataset. Your own honest reading is what is being collected. Matching an imagined majority produces worse work than simply answering as you see it.

The dollar figure is a floor, not a bonus. It exists so the value of your payment does not fall if the rupee weakens while you are working.

This is paid on completion, not per sentence

If you stop before the set is finished, there is no payment for the sentences you did answer. Someone who answers 9,000 and stops is paid nothing. Please take this seriously before you start: 15,000 sentences is real work spread over weeks, and the whole amount depends on reaching the end of it.

If something happens that is outside your control

Illness, a family emergency, anything genuinely unforeseen — that is not the same as choosing to stop, and it is not handled by a rule. Contact the project lead directly and it will be sorted out personally, case by case. Raise it rather than going quiet.

If the set grows past 15,000

Payment is capped at 15,000 sentences. Anything beyond that would be agreed separately before you are asked to do it.

Before you start

Confirm these with the coordinator

These follow from the terms above but are worth having in writing, so that nobody is working from a different assumption at the end.

  1. Which exchange rate settles the dollar comparison. Nepal Rastra Bank's published rate for the completion date is the natural neutral choice.
  2. What “completion” means — the day you finish your own last sentence, or the day all three of you are done. These can differ by weeks.
  3. When and how you are paid after finishing.
  4. What the quality-check threshold actually is — concretely enough that you know before you start what would cost you the payment.
  5. Whether your labels are published identified by annotator number in the released dataset.
